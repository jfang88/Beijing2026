# Great Wall Sections Near Beijing — How They Differ

This guide compares the Great Wall sections you can visit from Beijing and explains which one suits our [December itinerary](DecemberItenarary.md). Distances and drive times are approximate, measured from central Beijing in light traffic. Opening rules, lift schedules, and closures change often, so confirm them before you go. (Written November 2026.)

## The short version

- **Our pick for Mon, Dec 21: Mutianyu.** It's fully restored, has a cable car and a toboggan, is much quieter than Badaling, and pairs easily with the Ming Tombs in one day. That makes it the best fit for a family with a 10-year-old in winter.
- **Backup if Mutianyu's lifts are down or the weather turns:** go to **Badaling** (which has cable cars) or **Juyongguan** (closest to the city). Both are on the same northwest side of the city as the Ming Tombs, so the rest of the day still works.
- **Skip on this trip:** **Jiankou** is wild, unrestored, and not officially open. **Jinshanling** and **Simatai** are 2–3 hours away, steep, and icy in winter.

## Three kinds of wall

Almost every section you can visit near Beijing was built or rebuilt in brick and stone during the **Ming dynasty (1368–1644)**, often on top of older walls. The main differences between sections are how much they've been restored and how hard they are to reach.

| Type | What it means | Sections |
|---|---|---|
| **Fully restored** | Rebuilt walkways and towers, handrails in places, ticket centers, food, toilets, and usually lifts | Badaling, Mutianyu, Juyongguan |
| **Partly restored / original** | A mix of rebuilt stretches and original, weathered wall; fewer facilities and steeper stairs | Jinshanling, Simatai, Huanghuacheng, Gubeikou |
| **Wild (野长城, "wild wall")** | Unrestored ruins with loose bricks, overgrown paths, no rails or facilities; not officially open | Jiankou |

## Section by section

### Mutianyu (慕田峪): our choice

- **What it is:** About 2.2 km of restored wall with more than 20 watchtowers, winding along forested ridges in Huairou District. It's a Ming-era rebuild on older foundations. Unusually, it has battlements on **both** sides of the walkway, not just the outer side.
- **Getting up and down:** A shuttle bus runs from the visitor center to the lift area. From there:
  - **Cable car** (enclosed gondola) to **Tower 14**.
  - **Chairlift** (open) to **Tower 6**.
  - **Toboggan** down from **Tower 6**: a single-seat sled on a metal track, with a hand lever to control speed.
  - Walking up the stairs is also possible, but they're steep.
- **Lift operators:** The cable car and the chairlift/toboggan are run by **different companies**, and their tickets aren't interchangeable. The classic family combination is a one-way cable car up, a walk from Tower 14 to Tower 6, and a one-way toboggan down. The toboggan closes in rain, snow, or ice; if it does, take the chairlift down instead.
- **Crowds:** Busy in summer and on holidays, but far quieter than Badaling. On a winter weekday it can be nearly empty.
- **Why it suits families:** The lifts remove most of the climbing. The Tower 14 to Tower 6 walk is manageable for a 10-year-old. The toboggan is a highlight for kids, and there are good restaurants at the base.
- **Watch out for:** The cable car's annual maintenance day in December (Dec 13 in 2025). Lifts stop around 16:30 in winter. Steps can be icy.

### Badaling (八达岭)

- **What it is:** The most famous section and the first one opened to tourists (1950s), where visiting heads of state are usually taken. The wall here is wide, fully restored, and has handrails. It sits on a high mountain pass in Yanqing District.
- **Getting there:** About 1–1.5 hours by car. You can also take the Beijing–Zhangjiakou high-speed railway to **Badaling Great Wall Station**, one of the deepest high-speed rail stations in the world.
- **Getting up:** Cable cars, plus walking routes. The north side is the classic route and also the busiest.
- **Crowds:** The busiest section by far. You must book in advance with your real name, and there's a daily visitor cap. Winter weekdays are much calmer.
- **Best for:** First-time visitors, travelers with limited mobility, and anyone without a private car.
- **Watch out for:** Tour-group crowds, a more commercial feel, and an exposed, windy pass that feels colder than the city in winter.

### Juyongguan (居庸关)

- **What it is:** A fortified **pass** rather than a ridge-top wall. The wall climbs steep hills on both sides of a valley and loops around a fortress town. It's the closest major section to central Beijing.
- **Highlight:** The **Cloud Platform** (云台), a carved marble gateway from the Yuan dynasty (1340s) with Buddhist inscriptions in six scripts.
- **Getting up:** On foot only. The loop is short but extremely steep.
- **Crowds:** Moderate.
- **Best for:** A short visit, history, and pairing with the Ming Tombs, which are nearby in the same district.
- **Watch out for:** Relentless stairs and no lift, which is tiring for kids and harder on icy steps.

### Huanghuacheng (黄花城水长城): the lakeside Great Wall

- **What it is:** The only section where the wall runs straight into a reservoir. Part of it is underwater, so the wall seems to dive into the lake. The valley is known for its old chestnut orchards.
- **Getting up:** On foot; there's no cable car. Boat rides on the reservoir run only in the warmer months.
- **Crowds:** Low to moderate, busiest in spring blossom season.
- **Best for:** Photography, and a change from the classic ridge-top view.
- **Winter:** The frozen reservoir is striking, but there are no boats and the paths can be icy.

### Jiankou (箭扣)

- **What it is:** Wild, unrestored Ming wall on razor-edged ridges, named for its "arrow-nock" shape. Many of the most dramatic Great Wall photos come from here, including the "Beijing Knot" and "Eagle Flies Facing Upward" viewpoints.
- **Status:** **Not officially open to tourists.** It also has seasonal fire-prevention closures. One tour operator reports that a barrier was built at Tower 23 (the border with Mutianyu) in August 2025, ending the popular Jiankou-to-Mutianyu through-hike. I couldn't independently confirm this.
- **Best for:** Experienced hikers with a qualified local guide, in good weather only.
- **Not for this trip:** It's steep, crumbling, and dangerous in winter ice, with no rails or facilities.

### Simatai (司马台)

- **What it is:** Mostly original Ming wall, stabilized rather than rebuilt. It has famously steep stretches such as the "Heavenly Ladder" and the "Fairy Tower". You reach it through **Gubei Water Town**, a recreated northern water town at its foot.
- **Unique feature:** It's the only section regularly open **at night**, when the wall is floodlit and the water town is lit by lanterns.
- **Getting up:** Cable car plus walking. You need to book in advance, daily numbers are capped, and Gubei Water Town and the wall need separate tickets.
- **Best for:** An evening outing or an overnight stay in Gubei.
- **Winter:** Cold, and parts of the water town may be closed. Check whether night openings run in December.

### Jinshanling (金山岭)

- **What it is:** Ming wall in Hebei Province, rebuilt under General Qi Jiguang around 1570. It mixes restored and original stretches with many watchtowers and is a favorite with photographers.
- **Getting up:** A cable car takes you close to the wall, then you hike along the ridge.
- **Crowds:** Low, because the distance thins them out.
- **Best for:** Hiking, sunrise and sunset photography, and serious walkers.
- **Watch out for:** It's 2.5–3 hours each way from Beijing. The classic Jinshanling-to-Simatai through-hike has been closed for years.

### Gubeikou (古北口)

- **What it is:** A historic pass next to Simatai and Jinshanling. The wall here was left largely unrestored, and it was the site of fierce fighting against the Japanese army in 1933.
- **Best for:** History lovers, and hikers who want authentic wall without Jiankou's danger.
- **Watch out for:** No lifts, few facilities, and a long drive.

### Farther afield (not day trips)

- **Shanhaiguan and Old Dragon's Head** (Hebei, ~300 km): where the wall meets the sea. It's a few hours away by high-speed train and best as an overnight trip.
- **Jiayuguan** (Gansu, far west): the wall's great western fortress, built largely of rammed earth instead of brick. Getting there means a flight.

## Which section for whom?

| If you want… | Go to |
|---|---|
| The easiest family day, with lifts and a toboggan | **Mutianyu** |
| The most famous section, the easiest access, or a trip without a car | **Badaling** |
| The shortest trip from the city | **Juyongguan** |
| The wall meeting a lake | **Huanghuacheng** |
| A night visit | **Simatai** (with Gubei Water Town) |
| Hiking and photography with few people around | **Jinshanling** |
| Authentic, unrestored wall without extreme risk | **Gubeikou** |
| Extreme wild wall (experienced hikers only) | **Jiankou**, which is not officially open |

## Winter tips for any section

- **Grip:** Wear boots with good tread. Steps can be icy even on sunny days, and polished stone gets slippery. Small strap-on ice cleats are cheap insurance.
- **Wind:** The wall runs along exposed ridges, so wind chill makes it feel far colder than the air temperature. Bring hats, gloves, and neck warmers, and wear layers you can take off while climbing.
- **Daylight:** Sunset is around 16:50 in late December and lifts stop around 16:30, so start heading down by 16:00.
- **Weather closures:** Toboggans, some lifts, and the steeper sections can close for snow, ice, or high wind. Have a backup plan.
- **Food and water:** Kiosks on the wall are sparse in winter. Carry water and snacks; a thermos of something hot is a welcome treat at the top.
